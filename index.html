READING GUIDE

Evidence before interpretation.

This casebook follows the five categories assigned to the selected traces. Each walkthrough reconstructs a local sequence of observations, stated judgments, actions, and consequences.

Original evidence

Quoted task text, agent messages, tool output, or verifier results. Click a reference to inspect its location and surrounding source lines. JSON excerpts are decoded for readability.

Analytical interpretation

Our explanation of why a transition matters. These annotations are separate from the original log and do not claim access to unrecorded model reasoning.

Marked drift point

The earliest well-supported transition selected for this case. A temporary workaround or a single failed attempt is not, by itself, evidence of drift.

Recorded outcome

A failed result documents task performance. It does not establish that one highlighted behavior was the sole cause. Successful runs are illustrative comparisons, not controlled experiments.

Use it in a discussion

Keep the original objective visible. Advance one step at a time, inspect the evidence, then reveal the later result. Use ← and → in a walkthrough. Presentation mode keeps the current case and enlarges the reading surface; Esc exits.

Scope and counting

The source package contains 23 traces. These 10 walkthroughs focus on 9 selected traces; group 044, trace 001 appears under two categories because it contains two distinct transitions. The remaining runs supply context or comparisons. Repeated rows in exported tool tables can represent components of one command and are not counted as independent attempts.

Terminology

“Loop / repeated attempts” describes recurrence in a finite record. “Environment not updated” concerns failure to revise the working plan after new environment feedback. “LLM hallucination” covers unsupported assumptions or causal claims. “Forgetting constraints” is used as a category label; the evidence demonstrates a constraint being lost in implementation, not a directly observed mental state.
